Cambodia operates a self-assessment tax system administered by the General Department of Taxation (GDT) under the Ministry of Economy and Finance. For businesses operating in the Kingdom, understanding your tax obligations — and meeting them on time — is not optional. Non-compliance carries financial penalties and can jeopardize your operating license.

Overview of Cambodia's Tax System

Cambodia's tax administration is built on the self-assessment principle: businesses calculate and remit their own taxes to the GDT, with the GDT conducting audits to verify accuracy. The system distinguishes between three tax regimes based on business size and turnover — the Small Taxpayer Regime, the Simplified Regime, and the Real Regime. Most companies with formal operations, and virtually all foreign-owned entities, fall under the Real Regime, which carries the broadest set of monthly and annual obligations.

Businesses registered under the Real Regime are subject to corporate income tax, VAT, prepayment of profit tax, tax on salary, withholding tax, and the annual Patent Tax. Understanding each of these obligations — including their rates, filing deadlines, and interaction with each other — is fundamental to operating without penalties.

Key Business Taxes in Cambodia

Corporate Income Tax (CIT) — 20%

The standard Corporate Income Tax (CIT) rate in Cambodia is 20% on net taxable profit. This rate applies to most resident companies, including foreign-owned entities registered under Cambodian law. Certain sectors benefit from reduced rates: Qualified Investment Projects (QIPs) may receive CIT exemptions during a tax holiday period of up to nine years, while insurance companies are taxed at 5% on gross premiums. Companies must file an annual CIT return by March 31 of the following year, accompanied by audited financial statements prepared in accordance with Cambodian Accounting Standards (CAS).

Value Added Tax (VAT) — 10%

VAT is levied at 10% on the supply of goods and services within Cambodia and on imports. Exports are zero-rated, giving exporters the right to claim back input VAT paid on production costs. VAT-registered businesses must issue GDT-compliant VAT invoices for all taxable supplies. Invoices that do not meet GDT format requirements cannot be used to support input VAT claims.

Prepayment of Profit Tax (Minimum Tax) — 1% of Turnover

Businesses under the Real Regime must make a monthly prepayment of profit tax calculated at 1% of monthly turnover. These payments are credited against the final annual CIT liability. Importantly, even companies running at a loss must make this prepayment, which effectively creates a minimum tax floor of 1% of gross revenue. This monthly obligation — alongside VAT — forms the core of Cambodia's tax compliance burden for most businesses.

Withholding Tax

Cambodia imposes withholding tax on payments made to both resident and non-resident parties. Key rates include 15% on dividends paid to non-residents, 14% on interest paid to non-residents, and 14% on royalties and management fees paid to non-residents. Resident-to-resident withholding on service payments is 15%. These rates may be reduced under Cambodia's double tax agreements (DTAs) with countries including China, Singapore, Thailand, Brunei, and Vietnam.

Patent Tax (Annual Business License)

The Patent Tax is an annual business license fee payable to the GDT — not a tax on profits. It must be renewed before the start of each calendar year. The fee varies by business category, from approximately USD 50 for smaller businesses to several hundred USD for larger enterprises. Failure to renew the Patent Tax is a common compliance gap that can complicate government audits and banking relationships.

VAT Registration Requirements

Businesses with annual turnover exceeding KHR 250 million (approximately USD 62,000) are required to register for VAT with the GDT. Voluntary registration is also permitted below this threshold, which is often advantageous for businesses with significant input VAT from supplier invoices. To register for VAT, a business must already hold a Tax Identification Number (TIN) from the GDT.

The registration form requires details of business activities, turnover projections, and banking information. The GDT issues a VAT certificate that must be prominently displayed at the business premises. All VAT-registered businesses must issue GDT-compliant tax invoices for every taxable supply — invoices that do not meet GDT format requirements are rejected and cannot be used to claim input VAT credits.

Monthly Tax Obligations

Under the Real Regime, businesses face multiple monthly tax obligations, all due by the 20th of the following month:

Monthly Tax Filing Calendar — All Due by the 20th of the Following Month

  • VAT Return: Report output VAT collected and input VAT claimed; remit net liability to GDT
  • Prepayment of Profit Tax: 1% of monthly turnover; credited against annual CIT
  • Tax on Salary (ToS): Progressive rates withheld from employee wages; employer remits on behalf of employees
  • Withholding Tax Return: Any withholding tax deducted from payments made during the month

Missing any of these deadlines triggers an automatic 10% surcharge on the unpaid amount plus 2% monthly interest. The GDT has significantly increased its audit and enforcement activity since 2024, with electronic cross-matching of invoices making it considerably more difficult to conceal discrepancies between filed returns and actual transactions.

Tax on Salary (ToS) in Detail

ToS rates are progressive: salaries up to KHR 1,500,000 per month (approximately USD 370) are exempt from ToS. Rates then increase from 5% for the next income bracket up to 20% on the highest bracket. Employers must submit a monthly payroll declaration alongside the ToS remittance. Expatriate employees are generally subject to the same progressive rates as residents on Cambodia-sourced income, though applicable DTA provisions may provide relief in certain situations.

Annual Tax Filing

The annual Corporate Income Tax return must be filed with the GDT by March 31 of the following year. The return must be accompanied by audited financial statements prepared in accordance with Cambodian Accounting Standards. The audit must be conducted by a licensed Cambodian auditor or an internationally recognized firm with a Cambodian practice license.

The CIT return reconciles the monthly prepayment of profit tax payments made throughout the year against the final CIT liability. Where prepayments exceed the final liability, the excess may be carried forward as a credit against future tax. Refunds are theoretically available but are rarely granted in practice — most businesses carry excess credits forward.

Tax Incentives for Qualifying Businesses

Qualified Investment Project (QIP) Status

Businesses in manufacturing, agro-processing, tourism infrastructure, and other priority sectors may apply for QIP status from the Council for the Development of Cambodia (CDC). QIPs receive a CIT holiday of up to nine years, import duty exemptions on capital equipment and production inputs, and special depreciation allowances. The QIP application must be filed before the business begins operations and involves submission of a business plan, financial projections, and evidence of investment commitment. Importantly, companies with active QIP status still have annual filing obligations — failure to file during the holiday period can jeopardize incentive status.

Special Economic Zone (SEZ) Benefits

Businesses located in designated Special Economic Zones benefit from extended CIT holidays, reduced or zero import duties on raw materials and equipment, and streamlined customs procedures. Key SEZs include Phnom Penh SEZ, Sihanoukville SEZ, and Bavet SEZ near the Vietnamese border. SEZs are particularly relevant for export-oriented manufacturing operations.

Common Compliance Issues

The following are the most frequent tax compliance problems encountered by businesses in Cambodia, based on GDT audit findings and industry experience:

  • Late filing of monthly returns: Even a single day past the 20th deadline triggers the 10% surcharge. With four separate monthly returns, businesses without a structured compliance calendar frequently accumulate avoidable penalties.
  • Invalid VAT invoices: Input VAT claimed on invoices that do not comply with GDT format requirements will be disallowed during audits. This is a significant source of unexpected tax assessments, particularly for businesses sourcing from informal suppliers.
  • Transfer pricing: Transactions between related parties must be conducted at arm's length market rates. The GDT has increased its focus on transfer pricing since 2021, and businesses with intercompany loans, management fees, or royalty payments face heightened scrutiny.
  • Undeclared income: Cash-based or informal revenue streams that do not appear in VAT returns but show up in bank statements create significant audit exposure. Electronic payment flows are now routinely cross-referenced by the GDT.
  • Failure to file during CIT holiday: Companies with QIP status still have annual filing obligations during the holiday period. Failure to file can result in withdrawal of incentive status.
  • Record retention: All accounting records, supporting documents, and VAT invoices must be retained for a minimum of ten years. Inadequate records make it impossible to defend against audit assessments.
