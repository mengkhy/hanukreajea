Cambodia's 2026 Investment Law:
What Foreign Businesses Need to Know

Bodiatrade Team
March 15, 2026 • 8 min read

Cambodia's investment landscape is evolving rapidly. The 2026 Investment Law represents a significant shift in how the government attracts and regulates foreign capital, with profound implications for international businesses operating in or considering entry to the Kingdom.

Overview of the New Investment Framework

The 2026 Investment Law marks a comprehensive modernization of Cambodia's regulatory approach to foreign direct investment. After two years of consultation with business leaders, government agencies, and international partners, the law was designed to balance attracting world-class investors with strengthening labor protections and environmental standards.

The framework introduces a tiered incentive system that rewards businesses meeting specific criteria around technology transfer, local employment, and sustainability. Rather than broad tax holidays, companies now receive targeted benefits aligned with government development priorities. This shift reflects Cambodia's transition from a cost-focused to a value-focused economy.

"The new law signals Cambodia's commitment to responsible growth and partnership with ethical international investors," notes the Ministry of Commerce statement accompanying the legislation.

Key Changes Affecting Foreign Businesses

Enhanced Due Diligence Requirements

All foreign investors must now complete expanded due diligence procedures covering environmental impact, labor practices, and beneficial ownership transparency. This process typically takes 4-6 weeks, compared to 2-3 weeks previously. Companies should begin preparation early and ensure all documentation is available in both English and Khmer.

Technology Transfer Incentives

Businesses committing to technology transfer arrangements with local partners can access enhanced incentive packages. This includes reduced corporate tax rates (down to 15% from the standard 20%) and accelerated depreciation allowances. Documentation of technology partnerships must be formalized in signed agreements and submitted with investment applications.

Labor and Employment Standards

The law now mandates that foreign investors hire and develop local talent at all management levels. Companies must submit workforce development plans demonstrating pathways for Cambodian employees to advance into supervisory and executive roles within five years. Non-compliance can result in incentive reductions or withdrawal.

Environmental and Social Governance

All investment projects must now obtain environmental clearance from the Ministry of Environment before receiving final investment approval. Standards align with ASEAN guidelines and include mandatory annual sustainability reporting for the first five years of operation. Investors in renewable energy, sustainable agriculture, and green manufacturing receive additional tax benefits.

Sector-Specific Implications

Agriculture and Food Export

Agricultural businesses benefit from the most generous incentive package in the new law. Companies processing Cambodian agricultural products for export receive reduced corporate tax (15%), import duty exemptions on processing equipment, and accelerated depreciation. Quality certification compliance (ISO 22000, GlobalGAP, etc.) is now a prerequisite but provides significant cost advantages. Manufacturing and Special Economic Zones

Manufacturing companies in designated Special Economic Zones maintain preferential treatment but now face new local content requirements. Companies must source at least 30% of inputs locally within three years, increasing to 40% by year five. This creates opportunities for supply chain development and local partnership arrangements.

Technology and Services

Technology firms and business services companies benefit from expedited licensing processes and reduced registration fees. However, data localization requirements now apply to cloud service providers and digital platform operators, necessitating local server infrastructure or formal data processing partnerships with Cambodian entities.

Compliance Requirements and Timeline

The investment approval process now operates on a structured timeline with clear milestones. Initial application review takes 2 weeks, due diligence takes 4-6 weeks, and final approval takes 1-2 weeks. Companies must prepare comprehensive documentation including:

  • Detailed business plan and financial projections
  • Environmental and social impact assessments
  • Organizational chart with management team backgrounds
  • Technology transfer or partnership agreements (if applicable)
  • Labor and workforce development plan
  • Ownership structure and beneficial ownership documentation
  • References from previous investments in other countries

The Ministry of Commerce now offers a single-window service for investment registration, coordinating with other agencies. This centralization reduces time and complexity compared to the previous multi-agency approach. Looking Ahead

The 2026 Investment Law represents Cambodia's evolution as a destination for quality foreign investment. Rather than competing on cost alone, the Kingdom is positioning itself as a partner for long-term, responsible business growth. For international companies with genuine commitment to the Cambodian market and sustainable operations, the incentives are more attractive than ever.

The key to success is early engagement with qualified advisors who understand both the letter and spirit of the law. Companies that invest time in proper preparation and compliance typically experience smoother approvals and better long-term outcomes.
